About the project

Radar Alert Mapper turns a radar detector into a shared road-safety map. Like a Waze report, a red spot means "drivers, slow down here." Built as a first-year engineering research project at the University of Detroit Mercy.

How it works

1 · DetectUniden R7

The radar detector picks up K and Ka band radar and shows the band, exact frequency, strength and direction.

2 · ReadCamera

A Raspberry Pi HQ Camera on a 3D-printed mount watches the R7's screen and reads it with image processing and OCR.

3 · LocateGPS

A USB GPS adds the exact position and time to every alert.

4 · FilterRaspberry Pi 5

Python code drops false alerts: Q-Ride (the R7's own false-alert mode), store doors and repeat spots.

5 · ShareThis map

Whenever the Pi has Wi-Fi it uploads new alerts here by itself, every 5 minutes.

Reading the map

  • Heat: green = one alert, red = many. Red areas are where to slow down.
  • Ka (red) and K (amber): the two main radar bands used on the road. K also comes from some automatic store doors, which the filters try to remove.
  • Car arrow: which way I was driving (the R7's compass).
  • Shaded wedge: which side the radar came from, using the R7's direction arrow (ahead, behind or to the side).
  • Confirmed: a spot seen on two or more different drives.
  • Signal peak: tap an alert, then "Show where the signal peaked". The strongest reading is about where the radar was.
  • Same radar again: the same exact frequency seen again in the same place is probably the same radar unit.
  • Crashes (purple squares): City of Detroit crash reports for the last two years, from Detroit's open data. Darker = more crashes.
  • Heads-up mode: open the site on your phone before driving. It uses your GPS and says "slow-down zone ahead" out loud.

Privacy and safety

Alerts near where I live are never uploaded. My speed is not shown anywhere on this site, and it is blacked out of the photos.

The project's goal is safer driving: knowing where radar is used is a reminder to watch your speed there.
